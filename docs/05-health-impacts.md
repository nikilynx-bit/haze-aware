# Health Impacts of Haze in Singapore

## 1. Pollutant Profile of Haze Episodes

During transboundary haze events, PM₂.₅ dominates the health signal. Co-pollutants include:
- CO, NO₂, SO₂ (combustion)
- VOCs and PAHs (incomplete biomass burning)
- O₃ (secondary photochemistry, often elevated downwind)
- Ultrafine particles (< 0.1 µm) – less well monitored

## 2. Acute Health Effects (Epidemiological Evidence)

| Outcome | Typical Effect Estimate | Key SG Studies |
|---------|------------------------|----------------|
| Respiratory hospital admissions | ~2–6 % per 10 µg/m³ PM₂.₅ | Goh et al. 2019; Lim et al. 2012 |
| Asthma ED visits | ~3–8 % per 10 µg/m³ PM₂.₅ | Various SGH / KK H data |
| Cardiovascular admissions | ~1–3 % per 10 µg/m³ PM₂.₅ | Halonen et al. (multi-city incl. SG) |
| All-cause mortality | ~0.5–1.5 % per 10 µg/m³ PM₂.₅ | Time-series analyses |
| Eye irritation, conjunctivitis | Qualitative / syndromic surveillance | Polyclinic data |
| Skin irritation (eczema flare) | Emerging | Ongoing SG dermatology studies |

## 3. Sub-Acute and Chronic Concerns

- **Prolonged exposure (> 2 weeks of unhealthy PSI):** Potential for sustained airway inflammation; limited cohort data in SG.
- **Pregnancy outcomes:** Preterm birth, low birth weight – evidence from 2015 episode (ongoing analyses).
- **Mental health:** Anxiety, reduced quality of life during prolonged haze; limited SG-specific data.

## 4. Vulnerable Populations

- Children (< 12 yr) – higher ventilation rate per body mass.
- Elderly (> 65 yr) – pre-existing cardiorespiratory disease.
- Outdoor workers (construction, maritime, hawker centres).
- Individuals with asthma, COPD, ischaemic heart disease.
- Pregnant women.

## 5. Exposure–Response Considerations

- PM₂.₅ from peat smoke may have different toxicity per µg/m³ than urban PM₂.₅ (higher organic carbon, levoglucosan, PAHs) – active research area.
- Indoor infiltration factor in Singapore HDB flats and offices: ~0.6–0.8 (air-conditioned buildings lower this).
- Behavioural modification (staying indoors, N95 use) attenuates realised exposure – important for epi interpretation.

## 6. Healthcare-System Response

- NEA issues **Haze Health Advisories** at PSI thresholds.
- MOH activates surge plans at hospitals/polyclinics during Very Unhealthy / Hazardous episodes.
- Schools may suspend outdoor activities (MOE circulars).
- Employers advised under WSH guidelines to reduce outdoor work.

## 7. Public-Health Communication Metrics

- **PSI (Pollutant Standards Index):** Composite of 6 sub-indices; 24-hr PSI driven by the highest sub-index.
- **PM₂.₅ 24-hr mean:** More directly health-relevant; NEA publishes both.
- **Risk communication:** "Good / Moderate / Unhealthy / Very Unhealthy / Hazardous" bands with activity guidance.

## 8. Research Gaps

- Long-term cohort effects of repeated haze exposure in Singapore.
- Differential toxicity of peat-smoke PM vs. urban PM.
- Effectiveness of N95 / surgical masks in reducing personal exposure during SG haze.
- Indoor-air-quality interventions (HEPA purifiers) and measurable health benefit.
- Mental-health and well-being impacts.
- Vulnerability mapping at sub-district (planning area) level.
