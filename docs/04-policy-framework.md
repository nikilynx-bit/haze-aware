# Policy and Governance Framework

## 1. Domestic Legislation (Singapore)

### 1.1 Environmental Protection and Management Act (EPMA)
- General air-quality standards.
- NEA enforcement powers over local emission sources.

### 1.2 Transboundary Haze Pollution Act 2014 (THPA)
- **Extraterritorial reach:** Allows Singapore to pursue civil and criminal liability against entities (including foreign companies) whose activities cause or contribute to haze in Singapore.
- **Key provisions:**
  - Duty of care for Singapore-linked entities involved in land clearing.
  - NEA may issue notices requiring preventive / mitigative action.
  - Fines up to SGD 100,000 per day of non-compliance (capped at SGD 2 million).
- **Enforcement track record:** Limited; first enforcement actions post-2015. Criticised for evidentiary and jurisdictional challenges.

### 1.3 National Environment Agency Act
- Establishes NEA's regulatory mandate.

## 2. Bilateral and Sub-Regional Arrangements

- **Singapore–Indonesia bilateral cooperation** (various MOUs on fire monitoring and hot-spot sharing).
- **Indonesia–Malaysia–Singapore** trilateral discussions (ad hoc).

## 3. ASEAN-Level Instruments

### 3.1 ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution (AATHP, 2002)
- Legally binding; entered into force 2003.
- Obligations: monitoring, prevention, preparedness, emergency response, information exchange.
- **ASEAN Peatland Management Strategy (2006, revised).**
- **ASEAN Coordinating Centre for Transboundary Haze Pollution Control (ACC THPC)** – based in Indonesia.

### 3.2 ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre (ASMC)
- Hosted by Singapore (under NEA/MSS).
- Provides daily fire danger rating, hotspot monitoring, and haze outlooks for ASEAN.

### 3.3 ASEAN Haze-Free Roadmap (2025 target → revised)
- "Haze-free ASEAN" aspiration; progress reviews.

## 4. Indonesian Domestic Policy (Source-Side)

- **Inpres (Presidential Instructions)** on peatland moratorium (2011, extended).
- **PP 57/2016** on peatland protection.
- **KLHK fire-management regulations.**
- Enforcement remains uneven; provincial capacity varies.

## 5. International / Multilateral Links

- **UNFCCC / Paris Agreement:** Peat-fire CO₂ emissions relevant to Indonesia's NDC.
- **Convention on Biological Diversity:** Peatland biodiversity.
- **WHO Air Quality Guidelines (2021):** PM₂.₅ annual 5 µg/m³, 24-hr 15 µg/m³ – benchmark for Singapore standards.

## 6. Singapore Air-Quality Standards (NEA)

| Pollutant | 24-hr Standard | Annual Standard |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| PM₂.₅ | 37 µg/m³ (target) | 12 µg/m³ |
| PM₁₀ | 150 µg/m³ | 50 µg/m³ |
| SO₂ | 125 µg/m³ | – |
| NO₂ | 113 µg/m³ | – |
| O₃ (8-hr) | 160 µg/m³ | – |
| CO (8-hr) | 10 mg/m³ | – |

*Note: Singapore's PSI bands (Good ≤ 50, Moderate 51–100, Unhealthy 101–200, Very Unhealthy 201–300, Hazardous > 300) are a communication tool, not a health-based standard per se.*

## 7. Research Gaps in Policy

- Effectiveness of THPA as a deterrent (limited empirical evaluation).
- Cost–benefit of peat re-wetting vs. enforcement.
- Governance of supply-chain accountability (Singapore-linked companies in Indonesian concessions).
- Integration of climate-adaptation policy with haze mitigation.
