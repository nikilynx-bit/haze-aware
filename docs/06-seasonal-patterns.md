# Seasonal and Interannual Patterns

## 1. Singapore Climate Context

| Season | Months | Wind | Relevance to Haze |
|--------|--------|------|--------------------|
| Northeast Monsoon (NE) | Dec – Mar | NE → SW | Can carry Malaysian emissions southward; wet phase suppresses fire |
| Inter-monsoon I | Apr – May | Variable, light winds | Local stagnation events |
| Southwest Monsoon (SW) | Jun – Sep | SW → NE | **Primary haze transport window** (Sumatra → SG) |
| Inter-monsoon II | Oct – Nov | Variable | Transition; occasional Kalimantan smoke |

## 2. Fire Seasonality in Source Regions

- **Sumatra:** Two peaks – (i) Jun–Aug (dry season under SW monsoon), (ii) Feb–Mar (dry spell during NE monsoon). Riau peat fires peak Jun–Aug.
- **Kalimantan:** Aug – Nov, peaking around Sep–Oct.
- **Peninsular Malaysia:** Feb – Mar (dry spell) and occasional Jun–Jul.

## 3. ENSO Coupling

| ENSO Phase | Effect on Regional Dry Season | Haze Implication |
|------------|-------------------------------|------------------|
| El Niño (warm) | Reduced rainfall, extended dry season (especially Jul–Oct) | More fires, higher AOD, severe haze |
| La Niña (cool) | Above-average rainfall | Suppressed fire activity |
| Neutral | Average conditions | Baseline seasonal haze risk |

- Strong El Niño years (1997–98, 2015–16) produced the worst Singapore haze on record.
- Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) positive phase can compound dryness over Indonesia.

## 4. Diurnal Cycle

- Fires are often ignited in late morning / early afternoon.
- Sea-breeze circulation around Singapore can trap pollutants in the afternoon.
- Boundary-layer height lowest in early morning → elevated ground-level PM if smoke layer descends overnight.

## 5. Interannual Variability (PSI / PM₂.₅)

- Long-term NEA data show a declining trend in baseline PM₂.₅ since the early 2000s (local emission controls), punctuated by episodic spikes in major haze years.
- Frequency of "Unhealthy" (PSI > 100) days per year: highly variable (0 in clean years, > 30 in 2015).

## 6. Climate-Change Projections

- IPCC AR6: SE Asia likely to see increased frequency of extreme dry events under SSP3-7.0 / SSP5-8.5.
- Peatland degradation continues; drainage for agriculture increases baseline fire risk regardless of rainfall.
- Potential for longer fire seasons and more intense episodes – active research area.

## 7. Analytical Approaches for Seasonality

- **Wavelet analysis** of daily PM₂.₅ / fire counts to identify periodicities.
- **Composite analysis** by ENSO phase and monsoon season.
- **HYSPLIT trajectory clustering** stratified by month to visualise dominant transport corridors.
- **Generalised Additive Models (GAMs)** with cyclic splines for seasonality in epi time-series.
