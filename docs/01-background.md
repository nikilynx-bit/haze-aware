# Background: Haze in Singapore

## 1. What Is "Haze" in the Singapore Context?

In Singapore, *haze* refers to a suspension of fine particulate matter (primarily PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀) and gaseous pollutants that reduces visibility and degrades air quality. While the term can apply to any aerosol loading event, public and policy discourse in Singapore overwhelmingly associates "haze" with **transboundary smoke** from biomass and peatland fires in Indonesia (Sumatra and Kalimantan) and, to a lesser extent, Peninsular Malaysia.

## 2. Source Regions and Mechanisms

| Source | Mechanism | Typical Period |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Central & South Sumatra (Riau, Jambi, South Sumatra) | Slash-and-burn clearing for oil palm / pulpwood; peat fires | Jun – Oct (SW monsoon dry season) |
| Kalimantan (West, Central, South) | Peatland drainage + fire for agriculture | Aug – Nov |
| Peninsular Malaysia (Johor, Pahang) | Agricultural burning, occasional peat fires | Feb – Mar (NE monsoon dry spell) |
| Local (Singapore) | Vehicular emissions, industrial output, construction dust | Year-round (minor contributor to "haze episodes") |

Prevailing wind direction during the **Southwest Monsoon (Jun–Sep)** transports smoke from Sumatra across the Strait of Malaya into Singapore. During the **Northeast Monsoon (Dec–Mar)**, winds can carry emissions from Peninsular Malaysia southward.

## 3. Why Peat Fires Matter

Approximately 20 million hectares of tropical peatland exist in Indonesia. When drained for plantation agriculture, peat becomes highly flammable. Peat fires:

- Smoulder at low temperatures for weeks to months, producing dense, persistent smoke.
- Release disproportionately high CO₂, CO, and fine PM relative to surface biomass fires.
- Are extremely difficult to extinguish without re-wetting.

## 5. Local Amplifiers

Even when transboundary loading is moderate, Singapore's own urban emissions (traffic, shipping, industry) can compound PM₂.₅ concentrations, especially under low-wind, temperature-inversion conditions common in the inter-monsoon periods.

## 6. Socio-Economic Significance

- Disrupts aviation (Singapore Changi, regional airports).
- Reduces outdoor labour productivity (construction, maritime).
- Increases healthcare utilisation (respiratory, cardiovascular, ophthalmic).
- Recurring diplomatic friction between Singapore and Indonesia.
- Drives public demand for real-time air-quality information.

## 7. Research Landscape in Singapore

Key institutions producing haze-related research include:

- **National Environment Agency (NEA)** – monitoring, forecasting, enforcement under THPA.
- **NUS (Dept of Geography, Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health, Dept of Civil & Environmental Engineering)** – atmospheric modelling, epidemiology.
- **NTU (Asian School of the Environment, Earth Observatory of Singapore)** – remote sensing, peat science, climate coupling.
- **Meteorological Service Singapore (MSS)** – meteorological analysis, ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre (ASMC).
- **Singapore-MIT Alliance for Research and Technology (SMART)** – sensor networks, urban air quality.
